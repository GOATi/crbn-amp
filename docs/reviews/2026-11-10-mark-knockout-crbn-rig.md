# Knock-out search: "CRBN Rig" (2026-11-10)

**For:** the owner and his IP adviser, who decide (crbn-launcher D-RIG-004, D-CRBN-018 (c)). **By:** the RIG session on
Box-C. **What it is:** a knock-out search through web search and the trade-mark aggregator sites it surfaces (Justia,
Markinton, Trademarkdir, IP Australia's search pages). The registers' own systems (USPTO TSDR, EUIPO eSearch, WIPO
Global Brand Database, IP Australia ATMOSS) were not queried directly, and two aggregator pages refused or were
unreachable. **This is not legal advice and not a clearance search;** it is meant to catch an obvious conflict early.

## Findings

| 7 | "CRBN Rig" | - | - | Nothing found anywhere | No identical mark found |

In guitar amp simulation, the only "Rig" brand in the market is Native Instruments' Guitar Rig; Kemper uses "Rig" for
its profiles and their manager. "Rig" alone is everyday guitarist's language (a player's whole setup), which makes it a
weak word to claim on its own and suggests the combined "CRBN Rig" is what would be assessed.

## Questions for the owner or his adviser

1. **CRBN versus Audeze's CRBN (headphones, Class 9, US):** does the suite's prefix need clearing for audio software in
   the markets it will sell in (the US first), and should GOATi file "CRBN" or each "CRBN <Name>" itself? This is the
   suite's question, not Rig's alone.
2. **CRBN Rig versus Guitar Rig:** is "CRBN Rig" for amp-simulation software too close to Native Instruments' Guitar Rig
   in the EU, Germany or Australia, where NI's registrations, if any, were not reached here?
3. **Registers to search properly:** USPTO, EUIPO and DPMA (NI is German), IP Australia (GOATi's home), WIPO's Global
   Brand Database, in Class 9 (software, audio) and Class 42 (software services) at least.
