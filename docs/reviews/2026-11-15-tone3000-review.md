## What CRBN Rig is

A free amp and pedal player built on Neural Amp Modeler (Steven Atkinson's MIT code). It runs as a web page,
https://rig.carbonlinear.com, and as a Windows desktop app installed through our launcher, Pavillion Hub. Both are the
same page over the same NAM engine (WebAssembly in the browser, native in the app). Players keep their `.nam` captures
and `.wav` IRs in a folder of their own, which Rig lists as its library.

## How to try it

[Once published:] open **https://rig.carbonlinear.com/?tone3000** in Chrome or Edge. Until you sign it off, the TONE3000
tab shows only in a browser that opened Rig with `?tone3000`; nobody else sees it. In the library, choose
**TONE3000**, then **Continue**. To keep what you play, choose a folder under **My folder** first.

## Signing in, and the person's data

- OAuth 2.0 authorization code with **PKCE (S256)** and a fresh `state` every time. The publishable key
  `t3k_pub_J6MuMOba14a05Nl3Luf4Mp1Zj7Ik7oPi` is the `client_id`. Rig is a public client: **the secret key is never
  used or stored anywhere**.
- **Web:** a popup to your authorize page, back to `https://rig.carbonlinear.com/tone3000.html` (the only redirect
  registered on the key), which hands the answer to the page and closes. If the browser blocks popups, the same flow
  runs as a full-page redirect.
- **Desktop app:** your page opens in a window of the app's own (WebView2, owned by Rig's window), as your JUCE example
  does. Its redirect, `http://127.0.0.1/callback`, is caught **before any request is made** and handed to the page.
  The window only ever shows https pages; any other link opens in the person's browser.
- Tokens are kept on that person's computer only (the page's local storage). Rig refreshes 60 seconds before expiry,
  one refresh at a time, and keeps the rotated refresh token. **Sign out** forgets the tokens and the user's name. Rig
  has no server: nothing about the person reaches GOATi.

## Downloads, credit and offline play

- A model is fetched **only when the person asks**: Play in a tone's details, or a tone they picked in your picker. No
  prefetching; tone images load only as they scroll into view.
- `model_url` is fetched with the person's bearer token. Rig asks with `architecture=2`, plus A1 or custom where a
  tone has them, and plays all three. The file plays at once, exactly as you sent it.
- With a library folder chosen, Rig **keeps the file in that person's own folder**:
  `TONE3000/<creator> - <tone title> (<tone id>)/<model name>.nam` (or `.wav`). Beside it goes a `tone3000.json` with
  the tone's id, title, URL, gear, format and licence, the creator (username, display name, profile URL, avatar) and
  each kept model's id. Rig shows that credit wherever the file appears, offline too. A tone already kept plays from
  the folder and is not fetched again. **The file is never uploaded, shared, or passed to anyone else.** Without a
  folder, the capture plays for that visit only.
- Creator names, metadata and licences are never stripped or changed. Rig writes nothing into the files themselves.

## Calls and limits

Every call follows a person's action: a tab, a page, a search, opening a tone (`/tones/{id}`, `/models`), Play (one
`model_url`), a heart (`PUT`/`DELETE /tones/{id}/favorite`).

- **Search** waits until the person stops typing (450 ms) and has typed at least two characters.
- A **limiter** keeps each person under 60 requests a minute. A 429 says "try again in a minute".
- There is no polling, no background refresh, and no `/models/lookup` sweep of a person's folder.

Our automated tests never call your API: they run against a recorded copy of its answers, with made-up tones and
creators and NAM Core's example files. The one exception is the desktop test. It opens your authorize page once with a
deliberately malformed challenge, so the app can prove it catches your redirect. Your server rejects that at once, and
no one signs in.
